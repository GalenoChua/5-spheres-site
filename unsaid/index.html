Sphere 3 · Facilitator briefing

The Unsaid Protocol

A machine listens to the whole room for the whole session and tells you one thing nobody has said. That is all it does. Everything that makes it worth having is yours.

1. The goal, and the point

A roundtable runs ninety minutes with up to twelve countries at the table. The facilitator is managing turn-taking, watching the room, holding the thread and deciding who speaks next. Under that load the one thing no person reliably catches is an absence: the thing that should have come up and never did.

A table can spend ninety minutes on cross-border trade without one delegation saying the word payment. You cannot notice that while you are running a room. A transcript can.

The point is not a summary. There is no value in a machine reporting what was just said. The value is in the gap.

2. The mechanism

Two wireless microphones travel to whoever is speaking. A laptop beside the facilitator records, transcribes, and every minute writes a fresh reading from the whole session so far rather than the last few minutes, because the things worth surfacing live across a session instead of inside a window.

  • 1The co-facilitator reads it on their screen.
  • 2They decide: send it to the facilitator, or pass it over. Nothing reaches the facilitator unless a person sends it.
  • 3The facilitator puts it in their own words and chooses whether to use it at all.

Two people stand between the machine and the table. That is deliberate.

3. The tech, briefly

Transcription runs on the laptop itself. The audio goes to no company and does not leave the room. The reading is written by a language model, and before anyone sees it the claim is checked against the transcript as a plain string match: the quote has to actually appear, and the absent word has to actually be absent. A reading that fails that check is replaced by a warning.

And when the check fails

The reading is replaced by this warning and the questions are withheld. If you see it, do not say it. Press "Fresh reading" and carry on with the table.

4. The part that is actually the job

This is a Sphere 3 tool and it reaches exactly as far as the words go. Sphere 3 has three levels, and the reading touches two of them.

What it cannot see, and you can

Almost every Heart Question in Sphere 3 is about the body and the room. A transcript holds none of it.

  • Is their castle wall up, and is any part of your presence putting it there?
  • In the pauses, what is the energy filling the space? Tension, exhaustion, or hope?
  • When you mirror their posture, what does your body tell you that their words are avoiding?
  • Are you giving them enough silence to find their words, even when there has already been a pause?
  • Are you listening to win, preparing your rebuttal, or listening to believe?

The reading is the topsoil

The Field Guide is explicit that most people stop two or three layers too early and act on a surface feeling. Take what is on the screen as the first layer. Two things let you keep digging: space, holding several things on the table at once including ones that contradict each other, and curiosity, which is what stops you grabbing the first answer.

Two ways to put it to the table

"I hear you telling me this is fine, but I have a feeling it isn't. Am I reading that right?"

"As you said that, I noticed your hands tense up. Is this something that matters more than you are letting on?"

Half the time you will be wrong, and that is fine. The other person corrects you, and the conversation has still moved somewhere truer than it was.

What passing one over is for

Kahneman and Klein spent years on opposite sides of whether gut feeling can be trusted, and agreed on two conditions. Enough time in an environment regular enough to learn its patterns, and enough honest feedback to know when you were right and when you fooled yourself.

A roundtable gives you the first condition. The second is harder, because an ordinary roundtable ends without anyone finding out whether what you sensed was real. Every time you send a reading or pass one over, you record a judgement against evidence you can check afterwards. That is the feedback loop, and it is why this belongs inside the framework rather than beside it.

The three rules

  • Do not read it out as written, because it is a clue about the table and the table did not ask a tool a question. Put it into your own words before you say any of it aloud.
  • Check the quote before you speak, and if what you are about to say is not supported by that quoted line then leave it alone and carry on.
  • Give it ninety seconds and then stop, because explaining it to the table dilutes whatever it was worth in the first place.
