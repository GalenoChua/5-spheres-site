Tough Conversations: Giving Feedback

Somebody has to be told something they do not want to hear. Most of us soften it until it stops being true, or harden it until it stops being human. This is 110 minutes on saying the hard thing so the person in front of you still leaves feeling respected.

Why this one

You carry a badly handled conversation for years

I let people go during COVID. I was under-slept and running on empty, and I made it about me: my stress, my position, my guilt. I am still carrying that six years later, and so, I expect, are they.

That is the thing this drill is built around. The damage is rarely the news itself. People can take hard news. The damage is a person walking out thinking they only care about the business, not me, and that thought surviving in them long after the decision has stopped mattering to anybody else.

Who it is for

Anyone who has to deliver something the other person will not enjoy

Founders letting somebody go. Managers with an underperformer they have been avoiding for a month. Anyone who has rehearsed a conversation in the shower twelve times and still not had it.

You can bring a real situation you are carrying right now. You do not have to. There are scenarios, and they are there precisely so nobody has to put their own on the table in a room of people they have just met.

How the time runs

Theory, then you deliver it to a real person who scores you

It runs the same shape as the pitching and sensing drills: a short piece of theory, and then everybody practises it. You are put in a pair, where one of you delivers the feedback and the other receives it, and then the two of you swap over.

Both of you score it, and the receiver's score is the one that counts most. The question this whole session turns on is whether the person on the other side left feeling respected, and there is exactly one person present who can answer that.

Three scenarios, and you pick or bring your own:

  • Letting someone go.
  • Someone is underperforming.
  • Someone is underperforming, and is also dealing with something serious at home.
What you are taught

Five steps, and not one of them is a compliment sandwich

The sandwich is a way of making yourself comfortable while the other person leaves confused about whether anything was actually said. This starts somewhere else, from what you both already agree on.

  1. Start from shared purpose and common ground.
  2. Name clearly what has gone wrong, and the impact it is having.
  3. State the decision, or the change needed, plainly.
  4. Offer a way forward: what you recommend, what support exists, what happens next.
  5. Thank them for their time and their contribution.
Before you walk in

Settle yourself first, or you make the conversation about you

Physical, mental and emotional preparation, the same way the Sensing Drill handles it. You settle yourself, then you come back to why you are doing this at all, and only then do you open your mouth.

Skip it and you say the thing you will be apologising for in a year. This is Sphere 2, the Conscious and Physical Self, and it is the half of a tough conversation that happens before anybody speaks.

While you are in it

Their body is telling you something, and so is yours

Reading the other person is the obvious half. Watching what your own shoulders, hands and jaw are doing to them is the half people miss. We take both apart and then you practise with somebody watching.

What you leave with

A structure you can use on Monday, and a reading of how you did

Your own scores, the scores from the people watching, and the scores from the person who actually received it. Where those three disagree is the part worth your attention.
