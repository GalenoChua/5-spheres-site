The Method

The 5 Spheres of Empathy

Empathy is intelligence about people, starting with you. Not kindness, not sentiment, but a practical skill that helps you make better decisions, the kind that build something worth inheriting.

Most people treat empathy as a soft skill, a nice-to-have next to the hard skills that actually decide things. We treat it as a foundational discipline, one you practise. The 5 Spheres are five territories of attention, from the self you bring to work to the people who will inherit what you build. You can be working in any one of them right now.

How It Works

Five territories, not a ladder.

The spheres are not steps you climb and leave behind. They are a map, and on any given day, in any given decision, you are standing somewhere on it. The work is knowing where you are, and where the terrain asks you to go next.

The two inner spheres come first for one honest reason. Your own blind spots leak into everything downstream. Left unresolved, they quietly distort how you read your customers, your team, everyone. So you start by clearing your own view. From there, you go wherever the decision lives.

1

The Vulnerable Self

Why do I actually do this? Who am I really? What do I stand for?

The self work. Getting specific about what truly drives you, including the uncomfortable stuff most people are too busy to unpack. You cannot lead others if you are lost yourself.

2

The Conscious Self

What does care actually look like? What do I do differently on Monday morning?

The bridge between meaning well and doing good. Your capacity to show up, and the daily behaviours that turn your values into action, especially when you are tired, stressed and stretched.

3

The Direct Stakeholder

To what extent am I serving the people I want to impact?

Where your solution goes to die so a real impact can be born. Turning outward to the customer, the user, the team, and getting out of your own head and into the heart of what you are trying to solve.

4

Affected Indirect Stakeholders

What do my actions do to the people and places that never even see me?

The ripple effects. The child whose parent's eyes are averted, the supplier's family, the packaging that ends up under a park bench. The impact you never intended, and the opportunities hidden inside it.

5

Those Who Come After

What are we building for the people we'll never meet?

No feedback, no survey, no focus group. You are designing for a world you will never see. A society grows great when people plant trees whose shade they will never sit in. That is both the poetry and the design brief.

Why It Matters

Better empathy, better decisions.

This is not about being nicer. Empathy practised as a discipline changes what you can see, and what you can see is what you can decide well on. You stop building products that solve your own insecurity instead of the user's problem. You stop mistaking a values slogan for alignment. You catch the harm you never intended before it reaches the people you never meet. The clearer your empathy, the fewer expensive, avoidable mistakes you make. That is the whole return.

This is the method behind the workshops, the panels, and the cohort, and behind an average rating of 9.8 out of 10 from the people who have sat in the room.

The Body of Work

One framework, under everything.

The 5 Spheres sit on top of a wider foundation, the Business Sustainability First Principles, and run through all of it: the book, the workshops, the panels, the coaching. Wherever empathy travels, the same faculty does the work, whether you point it inward at your own values or outward at a generation not yet born.
