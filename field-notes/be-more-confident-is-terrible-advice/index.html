“Be more confident” is terrible public speaking advice

29 October 2026

They already know they need to be more confident. What they don't know is how.

At the end of my last workshop, I asked what people were taking away. Someone said: "You have to be more confident."

And I thought: shit, what have I done?

It is terrible advice and people (absolutely everywhere) tell me this is both what they hear all the time and what they tell others.

If they genuinely take the advice in, they end up feeling like something is wrong with them, because confidence isn't something you magically switch on.

If they don't take that advice, they switch off from you.

They already know they need to be more confident. What they don't know is how.

Here's what we do in our drills:

  • you write one message to convey,
  • then give a 90-second pitch,
  • the room scores you,
  • get the feedback,
  • go again (we didn't do that last time).

In that last session, one participant pitched ... but nothing landed.

I made a tough snap decision and chose for the group not to score it, my gut said there was nothing to score.

The other facilitators in the room could feel it too: she didn't have a message. I asked whether she'd written her sentence. "No. I couldn't."

Most people can tell immediately when you don't know what you're trying to say.

So I asked her what she'd tell a friend who was scared of speaking. And with no hesitation:

Everybody will forget what you say today. So just say what you want to say.

She could give away advice but couldn't use it herself!

Firstly, kudos for her to say this so openly.

Here's where it got interesting!

Jia Hong, an experienced investor and public speaker, who was also co-facilitating, threw out his prepared pitch and gave it in Bahasa instead! To be clear, he's not fluent in Bahasa Indonesia, he challenged her directly to step outside her comfort zone.

And to her utmost credit she took the challenge and went again:

Confidence doesn't mean being perfect. You just need to be brave enough to try. And be kinder to yourself.

Her scores came back as 'believable', 'clear' and most of all, 'confident'!

As we wrapped up, Jia Hong summarised it best:

Confidence is an outcome, not a practice. Let someone else decide about the confidence. I just want to know my point got across.

And that's where we start, focus on getting a point across, and once your confidence builds, then try different techniques to get the rest of your energy and intention across. One step at a time.

So instead of saying 'just be more confident', try:

  • "Write one sentence, one point that a stranger could repeat." Decide your point before you open your mouth because people know if you don't have a clear point.
  • "Too nervous for eye contact? Look at their foreheads." That was one of our participant's own tricks, and we think it's great!
  • "Why is this important to you?" Get their buy-in and help them see why it matters to them, and only then share your own public-speaking journey, if you have one. Buy-in beats advice.

All three work for someone who feels sick when they think about public speaking.
