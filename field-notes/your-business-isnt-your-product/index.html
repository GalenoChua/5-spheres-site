Your business isn’t your product

3 November 2026

Three years after ChatGPT 4, AI has done the complete opposite of helping us skip the hard human parts of business.

Three years after ChatGPT 4, it is now clear that AI has done the complete opposite of helping us skip the hard human parts of business.

And I flipping love it. We’re seeing the light now.

End of the tunnel nowhere in sight, but hear me out. 😅

AI has pumped out more competition for almost everyone in service.

  • From up and down your supply chain,
  • to ex-staff and colleagues,
  • your neighbour, their aunty.

Everyone can build virtually anything now. Oh, and it’s buffed up your competition too.

But.

It hasn’t made building a business any faster. Because your business isn’t your product.

AI makes building better, only if there’s quality human judgement.

AI improves user experience, only if the team has deep curiosity and intuition for the market.

AI closes deals faster, only if the sales team nurtures great relationships.

The list goes on and on, and it’s not limited to building a business. It’s virtually every sector that’s now AI-powered, and even AI-native.

AI multiplies the human judgement you already have. Or it exposes the lack of it.

So the companies missing the human side, the design, the empathy, the care for the person on the other end: they’re not hidden by AI. They’re now standing out more for all the wrong reasons. More noise. More slop.

The ones winning are the most empathetic. The ones who still understand who they’re building for.

The sale isn’t the finish line

“Even after a product is sold, real adoption can sit as low as 15%.”

That’s what Yev Nesin, Global Growth Strategy & Solutions Lead at Google Workspace, said on his panel at the Google for Startups campus last week.

The sale isn’t even close to the finish line in growth. Getting humans to actually use your product is, and it’s still painfully low.

I was there to keynote on why good strategies get stuck: the human side of AI, and almost all the speakers and panelists were landing on the same place.

Mariusz Gąsiewski, CEE Mobile Gaming & Apps Lead at Google, summarised it sharply. What used to take months now takes days, but growth is harder than ever. And the new moat is now ambition, appetite for risk, capital, and determination.

Notice what’s not on that list.

The tech.

Building is no longer the advantage now that everyone, their aunty and next door neighbour can do it. What’s left is the human stuff, with empathy being the key skill to growth.

The time did not disappear. It moved into the parts a machine cannot do for you.

Where this goes next

This is the conversation I’m most looking forward to having at the ASEAN Startup Connect in a couple of weeks.

In an AI-native world, the most human skills are the ones worth building.

This is what I’ve spent years mapping, and it’s the heart of the 5 Spheres of Empathy. The tools are live and the book is on the way.
