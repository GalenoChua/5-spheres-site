Why this lives here

Sustainability is empathy, made concrete.

Empathy is intelligence about people. Sustainability is what happens when you extend that intelligence to people you will never meet, and to a planet that cannot tell you what it needs.

That is not a metaphor. It is Sphere 4 and Sphere 5 of the method written out in full. Sphere 4 is the affected indirect stakeholders: the supplier three tiers down, the community beside the plant, the customer who inherits a decision made in a meeting they were never in. Sphere 5 is those who come after, who get no vote at all.

Almost every sustainability problem is a people problem. The technical answers are mostly known and already written down. What stops them is what stops everything else. Someone did not want to say the uncomfortable thing. The incentives pointed the other way. Nobody asked the person who would have to live with it.

So this course is not a detour from the method. It is the method applied where the consequences sit furthest from the room. Lesson 5 puts it plainly, that empathy is the superpower for exceptional leadership. Lesson 16 is all things people. Lesson 17 is all things planet. That is the whole argument, and the rest is how you actually do it.

The frameworks

What you will actually work with.

This is not a tour of theory. Each lesson hands you something you can put in front of a team on Monday, with the templates and reference sheets to go with it.

Clarity of vision

The Shannon-Weaver model of communication and Seth Godin's marketing promise, used to test whether a vision survives contact with the people meant to act on it. Most visions fail here, not at the strategy stage.

Key Performance Questions

A template for designing culture on purpose. You either design the relationships and culture you want, or you default into one you did not intend to have.

The Values to Behaviour Board

Values mean nothing until they are interpreted into action. This turns a wall of nouns into observable behaviour people can be held to, with a worked sample to start from.

Two roads into a roadmap

The commercial approach, for folding sustainability into a strategy that already exists, and the impact category approach for starting from impact instead. Includes a five year sample board and the Future Fit programme.

People and product journey mapping

The same journey mapping the Global Sustainability Tours run on. Map how a person moves through your organisation, and how a product moves through its life, then find where the leverage actually sits.

Diffusion of innovation

For internal change management, and the problem of getting buy-in when you are the one asking people to work differently. Comes with a worked HR policy sample.

Communicating without greenwashing

Regulatory guidance including the ACCC guidelines, worked through real enforcement cases from Coca-Cola, Shell in New Zealand, Procter and Gamble and others. What got them into trouble, and what to say instead.

Measurement and governance

Science Based Targets, what is worth measuring and what is theatre, and whether reporting is really necessary. The Carbon Almanac and Scaling Impact sit alongside as reference.
