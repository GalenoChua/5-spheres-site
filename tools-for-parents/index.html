Tools for Parents

Small tools, built for my own kids.

Teaching a seven-year-old to read a clock face takes more patience than either of you expects, and the apps that promise to help mostly want an account and an advert break. So I built one. It worked, so it is here, free, for anyone else sitting at a kitchen table with the same problem. More will follow as my children hand me the next thing they cannot do yet. There is also Frog Rock, which is mine and which is not a tool, and a way to help me make the next part of it.

Free tools

Open one, use it, close it

Nothing to sign up for, nothing to pay and nothing collected. Built because one of my children could not do the thing yet.

Telling the Time

Four levels, from o'clock and half past through to matching an analogue face against a digital one. The hands drag, so you can set your own questions by hand. English, Polish, Mandarin, Bahasa Indonesia and French, with pinyin you can switch off, and a history card for every hour on the clock.

Why these are here

I think parenting is the single biggest block on making the world better, and that is why I am doing this

That is a larger sentence than a clock app deserves and I know it. I work with leaders on reading a room, sitting with disagreement and staying present when leaving would be easier. Almost none of it starts at work. It starts with whether a child was listened to while they were getting something wrong, and whether the adult beside them stayed in the room.

What I am after is holding space. A child who has been held while they were confused learns that being confused is survivable, and most of what I teach adults is built on that ground or is trying to lay it late. Listening well is the thing a chief executive is paid for, and we mostly learn it or fail to learn it at about six.

The other half of it is what I would call human intelligence. Being deeply curious about another person. Noticing what they have not said. Carrying yourself in a way a child can copy. None of that arrives by being told. A child picks it up from sitting near somebody who does it, which is why I keep coming back to modelling rather than instructing. Mine have learned more from watching how I handle being wrong than from anything I have ever explained to them.

All of which is hard to do with nothing in your hands. Sitting down to teach a seven-year-old the clock gives you a reason to be there, a thing to look at together, and twenty minutes where you are both working on the same problem. The learning is real, and it is also the cover story. What the child remembers is that you sat down. So each tool here does two jobs: it helps your child with one concrete thing, and it buys you the time.

The last part is the part I cannot do alone. If you tell me roughly how long you sat together, and you only do that if you want to, I can start to see whether any of this holds. I am not claiming it yet. I need enough answers to build a study on, about what that time does to the children, to the parents, and to the human intelligence of both.

If you use one, tell me how it went. Three questions, no names, and the answers decide what I build next.

How these work

What is held, which is almost nothing

The tools themselves collect nothing. No accounts, no adverts, no third party trackers and no analytics on any of these pages. Nothing you or your child does in the clock app is sent anywhere or seen by me. It remembers two things, the level and the language you last used, and it keeps them in your own browser on your own device. Clear your browser data and they are gone.

Two forms on this page do store something, and both are ones you choose to fill in. The three question form stores the three answers and the time they arrived, with no name, no address and no identifier of any kind, which also means there is no way to connect two answers to the same parent. The Frog Rock list stores the email address you type, a first name if you give one, and one line if you write one. No card details are asked for anywhere on this site and none are held.

If something is broken or wrong, or if there is a tool your child needs that is not here, tell me and I will look at it. If you have used one with your child, the three question form is the most useful thing you can send me.
