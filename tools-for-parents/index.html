Tools for Parents

Free tools to help you spend real, learning time with your children.

Why this is here

I believe parenting is the single greatest lever we have for bringing more empathy into the world.

Children learn empathy earliest and deepest in their first years, long before school. Decades of developmental research point the same way: empathy and the urge to help appear in the first two years of life, and children build them largely by watching the people who raise them.

Sources: Decety & Cowell, on the early development of empathy; systematic review on attachment and prosocial behaviour in children.

They learn it by watching how the adults around them listen, stay patient, show kindness, handle being wrong, and generally just how they show up.

And those moments need one thing above all: time together.

Unhurried, device-down, side-by-side time. That is getting harder for every parent I know, myself included.

So I started making small tools that give me a reason to sit down with my own children and learn something together. A clock to read. A language to practise. Little things that turn ten minutes into connection.

This is the beginning of something bigger. If these help, I want to understand how, and eventually to study properly whether tools like these, and the time they create, really do grow empathy in families. That starts with you telling me whether it worked.

The tools

Telling the Time

An interactive clock to learn to read the time together, analog and digital. Four levels, from o'clock to quarter-past and beyond, with a little history fact for each hour. In English, Polish, Mandarin (with pinyin), Bahasa Indonesia and French.
