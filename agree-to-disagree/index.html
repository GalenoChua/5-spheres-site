What It Is

Everyone has a leadership philosophy, until a values-conflict or an emergency punches them in the face.

So we put four people from completely different worlds at one table, hand them difficult statements they have not seen before, and ask them to name what is actually broken.

It runs about an hour. It is free to attend when it sits inside a public gathering, and it travels to conference stages when the right one comes up. It has run in Warsaw, Gdańsk and Phnom Penh so far, hosted inside Venture Café Warsaw, Infoshare and the ASEAN Startup Connect Summit.

The Format

A roundtable, not a lecture.

Four panellists are handed difficult statements, blind. For each one, they take a side, then defend it in the room.

1

Agree. Say where you stand, honestly.

2

Disagree. Or take the other side, out loud.

3

Defend. Then hold your ground under pressure.

The room votes on every statement, before the panel gets to answer.

What It Stands For

Disagreement is not the problem. Pretending is.

The series lives inside the 5 Spheres of Empathy, and not off to the side, because empathy is not agreement. It is holding a perspective that is not yours long enough to actually understand it. You grow your empathy by growing your perspective, and there are few faster ways to do that than sitting across from four people who see the world nothing like you do and staying in the room anyway.

Boundaries around behaviours, not people. Before a single difficult word, the room agrees how it will treat anyone thinking out loud. That floor is what makes the rest survivable.

Nobody is safely on a stage. It is a table the room sits around, not a row of talking heads. You vote agree or disagree on every statement, and you get to say your own view.

Honest beats polished. The format gives everyone permission to say the real thing, including the panellists who change their minds halfway through.

Connection over winning. The point is not the cleverest line. It is the connection that holds after the disagreement.

From the Last Table
Good judgment has a shelf life.

On 9 July at Venture Café Warsaw, four leaders took on the greatest challenges in leadership right now. The statement on screen: your judgment was formed in a world that no longer exists, so you can no longer trust it. Marcin Dakowski and Prof. Ewa Bulska landed on the answer together. The danger is not being wrong. It is being too certain, when you are still changing and so is the world.

Jerzy Brodzikowski, on owning what you do not know: the person who admits "I don't know" is usually the one you can trust with the task. Celina Kretkowska-Adamowicz, on crisis: the bravest response is rehearsed long before it happens, so the team activates and runs without waiting to be told. Every truth of the night pointed back to the same job, which is preparing a space for people.
